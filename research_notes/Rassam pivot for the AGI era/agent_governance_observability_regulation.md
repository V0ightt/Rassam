## 1. Agent observability and visual agent builders: market traction, consolidation and gaps

### Takeaway
LLM/agent observability became a consolidation market in 2026. Database, APM and network incumbents bought the leading independents (Langfuse → ClickHouse, Arize → Dynatrace, Galileo → Cisco), while Braintrust raised money and stayed independent. Trace capture is turning into a commodity built on OpenTelemetry. The agent/multi-agent parts of the OTel GenAI conventions are still "Development" status, and surveys keep finding that most enterprises cannot see how agents decide, what they touched, or produce an audit trail. Tools record traces well. What's missing is a way to understand them: a human-legible picture of what the agent system is, what it can reach, and why it did what it did.

### Inferences
- Collecting traces (spans, tokens, cost, latency, evals) is being absorbed into incumbent observability and data stacks: Dynatrace, Cisco/Splunk, ClickHouse, Datadog. A small open-source project is unlikely to win as "another trace store".
- The unmet need in the surveys is not raw telemetry. It is **interpretation**: why an agent did X, what it was allowed to do, an inventory of agents, and an audit trail a non-specialist can read. That gap sits above traces, in topology, permissions and causality, and is where a "living map" product could fit.
- Because OTel agent conventions are still unstable, a tool that normalizes several trace formats into one stable, human-readable graph has a window of roughly 12–24 months before the standard settles.
- Visual builders such as n8n and Agent Builder-style canvases show that a flowchart-as-interface for agents has mainstream acceptance. They are *authoring* tools, though. They map the intended workflow, not the as-built, as-run system that includes code, infrastructure, credentials and emergent agent behavior.

### Gaps
- Search budget ran out before I could verify current (2026) traction, funding or user counts for LangChain/LangSmith, LangGraph Studio, OpenAI AgentKit/Agent Builder (launched Oct 2025 per prior knowledge, unverified here), Microsoft Copilot Studio, Dify, Flowise (reported acquired by Workday in 2025 per prior knowledge, unverified here), AgentOps, Datadog and New Relic LLM observability.
- I found no independent (non-vendor) survey quantifying "multi-agent causality" or "cost attribution across steps" as distinct pain points. The evidence is the vendor surveys above plus Gartner's citing of cost and risk controls (Section 5).

## 2. Regulation and standards: logging, transparency, human oversight (EU AI Act, NIST, ISO 42001, Colorado, US federal, UK)

### Takeaway
The EU AI Act remains the only binding horizontal regime that requires architecture-level documentation, automatic logs and human-oversight design for AI systems. The 2026 "Digital Omnibus" (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) **deferred** rather than removed high-risk obligations: Annex III moves to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I to 2 Aug 2028. The US went the other way: federal preemption pressure on state laws (EO 14365), Colorado's law stayed in court and rewritten for 2027, and voluntary NIST frameworks. ISO/IEC 42001 certification is growing but still in the hundreds of organizations.

### Cited Findings
**EU AI Act timeline after the Digital Omnibus**
- **[LAW IN FORCE]** The Digital Omnibus on AI is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. It was published in the Official Journal around 24 Jul 2026 and entered into force 27 Jul 2026. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/). Legislative path: provisional agreement 7 May 2026, Parliament vote 16 Jun 2026, Council final adoption 29 Jun 2026. — [Sidley Data Matters, 22 Jun 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/)
- New high-risk application dates: **Annex III stand-alone high-risk systems (employment, credit, education, etc.) move from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027. Annex I (AI in regulated products) moves to 2 Aug 2028.** — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Hogan Lovells/HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)
- Other omnibus changes: the AI-literacy duty (Art. 4) on providers and deployers is replaced by a Commission/Member State duty to "support and facilitate". The Art. 6(3) exemption keeps registration, now simplified. SME relief extends to small mid-caps. The AI Office supervises AI systems built on a provider's own GPAI model. The sandbox deadline moves to 2 Aug 2027. A narrower "safety component" definition applies. A new Art. 5 prohibition on AI generating CSAM or non-consensual intimate imagery applies from 2 Dec 2026. — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)
- **[LAW IN FORCE]** GPAI model obligations have applied since 2 Aug 2025 and are unaffected by the omnibus. Art. 50 transparency (telling people they are interacting with AI, labelling deepfakes) applies from 2 Aug 2026. Only the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking duty for generative systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 gets a grace period, to 2 Dec 2026. The penalty ceiling is unchanged at €35M / 7% of global turnover. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [AI Act implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)
- *Source conflict:* some trackers disagree on whether certain 2 Aug 2026 dates still apply and on the pre-omnibus Annex I date. CSA and Usercentrics both advise checking the OJ text. — [aipolicydesk](https://www.aipolicydesk.com/blog/eu-ai-act-august-2026-whats-delayed-vs-what-applies); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)

**What Arts. 11–14, 19, 26 and Annex IV require (text of Reg. (EU) 2024/1689; [LAW ADOPTED, application deferred to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028 for high-risk])**
- **Art. 12 Record-keeping:** high-risk AI systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system". Logging must record events relevant to (a) identifying situations that may present a risk or a *substantial modification*, (b) post-market monitoring (Art. 72), and (c) deployer monitoring of operation (Art. 26(5)). Remote biometric ID systems need extra fields: period of use, reference database, input data and the persons verifying results. — [AI Act Art. 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/)
- **Art. 19 / Art. 26(6) retention:** providers (Art. 19) and deployers (Art. 26(6)) must keep the automatically generated logs under their control for a period appropriate to the purpose, **at least six months**. — [AI Act Art. 19](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/19/); [AI Act Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)
- **Art. 13 Transparency:** high-risk systems must be "sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately". Instructions for use must cover capabilities and limitations, human-oversight measures, and (13(3)(f)) "a description of the mechanisms ... that allows deployers to properly collect, store and interpret the logs". — [AI Act Art. 13](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/13/)
- **Art. 14 Human oversight:** systems must be designed so that overseers can (a) understand capacities and limitations and monitor operation, including anomalies, (b) stay aware of automation bias, (c) correctly interpret output, (d) decide not to use, disregard, override or reverse the output, and (e) intervene or interrupt via a "stop" button or similar. — [AI Act Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)
- **Annex IV technical documentation (Art. 11)** must include design specifications and "key design choices including the rationale and assumptions" (2(b)). It must include **"the description of the system architecture explaining how software components build on or feed into each other"** (2(c)), an assessment of human-oversight measures and of the technical measures for interpreting outputs (2(e)), pre-determined changes and continuous-compliance solutions (2(f)), test logs (2(g)), and (point 6) "a description of relevant changes made by the provider to the system through its lifecycle". — [AI Act Annex IV](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/4/) (fetched 19 Nov 2026)
- I found no source saying the omnibus changed the *substance* of Arts. 12–14 or Annex IV. Sources describe only date shifts and simplifications. — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)

### Inferences
- The obligation that most closely matches a "living system map" is **EU AI Act Annex IV 2(c) plus point 6**: a description of the system architecture showing how software components feed into each other, plus a lifecycle record of changes. Arts. 12/19/26 add an operational log trail kept at least 6 months. This applies only to **high-risk** AI providers and deployers, mainly from **2 Dec 2027**. Most coding agents and general enterprise agents are *not* high-risk under Annex III unless used in listed domains such as HR, credit or essential services.
- The regulatory trend is "deferred, not cancelled" in the EU and "deregulate and preempt" in the US. US demand for maps will come mostly from security and operational incidents, insurance, and sector regulators, not from horizontal AI law.
- ISO 42001's small certified base (hundreds) shows management-system governance is still early. Organizations certifying need evidence artifacts (system inventory, impact assessments, monitoring records), which maps could produce.

## 3. Liability: Product Liability Directive, case law on AI/agent actions, financial-regulator guidance

### Takeaway
From December 2026 the EU's revised Product Liability Directive (PLD) makes software, including AI systems, a "product" under strict, no-fault liability, with evidence-disclosure duties and presumptions of defect that favor claimants. Authorship doesn't matter: whoever places the software on the market is liable whether a human or an AI wrote the code. No court has yet awarded damages for an autonomous agent's independent action. Courts in the US and EU are, however, consistently attributing AI system outputs to the deploying company, and banking regulators have reorganized model-risk guidance while explicitly leaving GenAI/agentic AI for later.

### Cited Findings
**EU PLD (Directive (EU) 2024/2853) [LAW ADOPTED; transposition deadline 9 Dec 2026]**
- Member States must transpose by **9 Dec 2026**. The new rules cover products placed on the market after that date. A corrigendum reportedly shifted the date to 8 Dec 2026. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/eu-product-liability-directive-where-things-stand-with-member-state-transposition/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/the-new-eu-product-liability-directive-eu-2024-2853)
- "Product" now includes software such as operating systems, firmware, applications and **AI systems** (Recital 13). — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://connections.nortonrosefulbright.com/post/102jpjp/revised-product-liability-directive-introducing-rules-on-strict-liability-for-ai); [Cleary Gottlieb, 2026](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/the-new-eu-product-liability-reform-addressing-the-digital-age)
- Transposition status: in June 2026 only Hungary had adopted a law. A later Reed Smith overview lists Hungary, Croatia and Lithuania. Germany is transposing 1:1 via a new Product Liability Act. Greece, Luxembourg, Malta, Portugal and Spain had shown no public progress. Germany's and Slovakia's drafts reportedly omit Art. 12(2) (recourse limits for small software-component makers). — [Jones Day, Jun 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/the-revised-eu-product-liability-directive-state-of-play-across-eu-member-states-and-evolving-risk-landscape); [Covington Inside Privacy, updated 17 Aug 2026](https://www.insideprivacy.com/european-union-2/eu-member-states-begin-rolling-out-new-product-liability-rules/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/eu-product-liability-directive-where-things-stand-with-member-state-transposition/)
- The Directive's text provides for court-ordered disclosure of relevant evidence by defendants and rebuttable presumptions of defectiveness or causation where technical or scientific complexity makes proof excessively difficult. Manufacturers stay liable for software updates and upgrades within their control. — [Directive (EU) 2024/2853, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj); [Cleary Gottlieb PDF](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/-/media/files/alert-memos-2026/eu-product-liability-reform-key-impacts-for-tech-companies.pdf)

**Case law on AI/agent actions**
- *Amazon.com Services v. Perplexity AI* (9th Cir., 4 Aug 2026): commentary reports the court treated the AI agent as "a tool, not a person for statutory purposes" and vacated a preliminary injunction on the statutory-access theory. Operator tort liability was left undecided. — [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/ninth-circuit-ai-agents-tools-not-persons-cfaa-august-2026/); [CompleteAITraining](https://completeaitraining.com/news/first-agentic-ai-appellate-ruling-gives-companies-a-roadmap/). *Secondary sources; opinion not read.*
- In May 2026 a German appeals court held a clinic operator responsible for false statements by its website chatbot and treated them as the company's own conduct. A Munich court enjoined Google over AI Overviews linking publishers to scams. Earlier: Air Canada was held responsible for its chatbot's misinformation (2024), and in the Workday hiring-tool litigation the AI vendor may be liable as an employer's "agent" (no final ruling). — [Praxikon, "AI agent liability: the case law in 2026"](https://www.praxikon.com/en/posts/ai-agent-liability-case-law)
- As of 15 Nov 2026, one review states there is **no ruling in Europe or the US ordering the maker or user of an autonomous AI agent to compensate damage the agent caused**. — [Praxikon](https://www.praxikon.com/en/posts/ai-agent-liability-case-law). The ABA describes an "emerging liability gap" for agentic AI (Oct 2026). — [ABA Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-october/agentic-ai-emerging-liability-gap-businesses/)
- *Conflict:* one source describes an unnamed 10 Aug 2026 appellate ruling holding a deployer partially liable for agent actions. I could not corroborate it. — [goodfault.com](https://goodfault.com/writings/liability-map-2026)

**Financial regulators**
- **US banks [SUPERVISORY GUIDANCE]:** On 17 Apr 2026 the Fed, OCC and FDIC issued **SR 26-2**, superseding SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8. It narrows the definition of a model and scales oversight to materiality. **Generative and agentic AI are out of scope pending an RFI**, and non-generative AI stays in scope. OCC Bulletin 2026-13 rescinds OCC 2011-12 and the MRM Handbook booklet. — [RocketDocs](https://rocketdocs.com/resources/blog/sr-11-7-replaced-model-risk-guidance); [The World of AI](https://theworldofai.org/ai-compliance/sr-11-7/); [Botminds](https://www.botminds.ai/guides/sr-11-7-and-generative-ai). *Secondary sources; primary Fed/OCC texts not opened.*
- **Singapore MAS [FINAL GUIDELINES, phased]:** Final Guidelines on AI Risk Management were issued 7 Oct 2026 after a Nov 2025 consultation. - **UK FCA:** see Section 2 (failure/near-miss reporting; Mills Review agent authorisation/identification). — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/briefings/2026/10/ai-in-financial-services-fca-good-and-poor-practice-and-the-road-to-agentic-finance)

### Inferences
- The PLD makes **documentation a litigation asset**. Defendants can be ordered to disclose evidence, and presumptions of defect hit firms that cannot explain complex systems. A maintained, versioned architecture and change map of AI-built software lowers that exposure, even though no law names "maps".
- Courts are converging on "the agent is your tool, its output is your conduct". Responsibility therefore stays with the human organization, which needs to show what its agents could do and did. That supports demand for permission and action maps more than for code diagrams.
- Bank regulators carving GenAI/agentic AI out of SR 26-2 "pending RFI" means US bank-specific agent rules are probably 2027+. MAS (2027–28) is the clearest near-term financial mandate that explicitly covers agents.

### Gaps
- I found no SEC or ECB guidance specific to AI in software development or agentic systems in this session (search budget exhausted).
- The status of the EU AI Liability Directive is unverified here (prior knowledge: the Commission withdrew it in 2025).
- No source addressed liability allocation specifically for *AI-generated code* (as opposed to AI outputs or decisions). The PLD's authorship-neutral strict liability is my inference from its scope, not from a ruling.

## 4. Software supply chain and provenance touching AI-generated code (SBOM, CRA, AI-BOM, SLSA, attribution, agent identity, MCP)

### Takeaway
The EU Cyber Resilience Act is now the hard anchor. Vulnerability and incident reporting has been live since 11 Sep 2026, and machine-readable SBOMs plus full security requirements apply from 11 Dec 2027. The US moved the opposite way: OMB rescinded the mandatory secure-software attestation in Jan 2026, and CISA updated SBOM minimum elements, while AI-specific SBOM guidance (G7, May 2026) is voluntary. Provenance of AI-written code has no legal standard. Conventions such as Linux's `Assisted-by:` trailer are contested and changing. Meanwhile, agent identity products shipped (Okta, Microsoft Entra Agent ID), and a wave of 2026 MCP incidents made "what can this agent/tool reach?" a security question.

### Cited Findings
**EU Cyber Resilience Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/2847)**
- **[LAW IN FORCE since 11 Sep 2026]** Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents for all in-scope products with digital elements, including products already on the market. The process has three stages (early warning, notification, final report) via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform. Breaches can draw fines up to €15M or 2.5% of worldwide turnover. — [Freshfields, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-obligations-take-effect-on-11-september-2026-102nzmk); [Skadden, Sep 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/get-ready-reporting-faqs-and-checklist); [European Commission CRA reporting](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)
- **[LAW ADOPTED, applies 11 Dec 2027]** The essential requirements (Annex I), conformity assessment and technical documentation apply from 11 Dec 2027, including drawing up an SBOM "in a commonly used and machine-readable format covering at the very least the top-level dependencies". Open-source stewards' reporting obligations also apply from 11 Dec 2027. — [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-eu-cyber-resilience-acts-vulnerability-and-incident-reporting-requirements-are-now-live/); [CRA text, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj); [Irish NCSC CRA page](https://ncsc.gov.ie/cra/)

**US federal software supply chain**
- **[POLICY CHANGE]** OMB **M-26-05** (23 Jan 2026) rescinded M-22-18 and M-23-16. **Provenance/attribution of AI-generated code (community conventions, [VOLUNTARY])**
- Linux kernel *AI Coding Assistants* policy (v7.0 docs): contributions carry `Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION [TOOL1] [TOOL2]` (example: `Assisted-by: Claude:claude-3-opus coccinelle sparse`). **"AI agents MUST NOT add Signed-off-by tags"** because only humans can certify the DCO, and the human submitter takes full responsibility. — [kernel.org v7.0 docs](https://docs.kernel.org/7.0/process/coding-assistants.html)
- The convention is unstable. v7.3-rc7 docs show a simplified `Assisted-by: LLM [TOOL...]`. **MCP security incidents (2025–2026)**
- On 30 Jun 2026 Microsoft researchers disclosed that MCP tool descriptions can be silently modified to steer agents into exfiltration **while each action still looks authorized in logs**. — [CSA research note, Jul 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-mcp-tool-description-poisoning-20260711-cs/)

### Inferences
- The CRA is the strongest binding provenance driver for anyone selling software in the EU. Every manufacturer needs component inventories (SBOM), vulnerability handling and technical documentation by Dec 2027 regardless of whether humans or agents wrote the code. AI-generated code adds volume and churn, which makes continuously regenerated documentation more valuable.
- No law requires labelling AI-authored code. Provenance is governed by community norms (kernel, Kubernetes) and vendor defaults (Co-Authored-By trailers emitted by coding agents), and those norms are diverging. A tool that *reads* all these signals rather than enforcing one is better positioned.
- The MCP findings show logs are not enough. Poisoned tool descriptions produce actions that look authorized in the logs. Reviewers need the configured **capability graph** (which agent → which MCP server → which credentials/tools → which environments) to judge whether an "authorized" action was appropriate.

## 6. Synthesis: which documentation/visibility artifacts are or will be mandatory, for whom, by when

### Takeaway
Binding requirements for system maps exist but are narrow and mostly EU-based. In 2027–2028 the most important are EU AI Act Annex IV architecture plus change documentation and Art. 12 logs (high-risk AI only, from Dec 2027 or Aug 2028), and CRA SBOM, technical documentation and vulnerability handling (all EU software products, from Dec 2027; reporting already live). ### Inferences
- **Strongest durable mandate for "maps":** AI Act Annex IV 2(c) literally requires describing how software components build on or feed into each other, plus a lifecycle record of changes (point 6). For any AI-heavy product that is high-risk, a continuously regenerated architecture and change map is close to a required deliverable from Dec 2027.
- **Broadest mandate:** the CRA covers essentially all commercial software sold in the EU. It requires inventories (SBOM), documentation and vulnerability handling, with reporting already live. AI-built code faces the same burden with more churn, which favors automated, always-current documentation.
- **What is not mandated anywhere:** agent permission/capability maps, AI-authored-code provenance, multi-agent causality views. These rest on incident-driven demand (PocketOS, MCP poisoning), insurer and procurement pressure, and sector supervisors (MAS, FCA). That demand is real but voluntary, and in the US the policy direction (OMB M-26-05, EO 14365) points toward *less* mandatory documentation through at least 2028.
- The hypothesis that organizations will be legally *and* operationally required to understand what AI-built and AI-run systems do is **partly supported**. Legal requirements are concentrated in the EU, slipped about 16–24 months, and are scoped to high-risk AI and product security. Operational need is supported by incidents and surveys, and liability trends (PLD, courts treating agents as the company's tool) keep responsibility with humans. The hypothesis overstates US legal compulsion.

- Not verified: the exact OJ text of Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 (date conflicts across secondary sources), harmonised standards for Art. 12 logging, and any EU guidance on whether agentic coding tools could fall under high-risk categories.

## Implications for Rassam

- **Reposition from "repo → diagram" to "evidence-grade living system map".** Build the artifacts regulators actually describe: an always-current architecture view of how components "build on or feed into each other" (AI Act Annex IV 2(c)), a versioned change log of the map (Annex IV point 6), and links to SBOM/dependency inventories (CRA, Dec 2027). Export these as dated, signed snapshots. Rassam's existing `CanvasSyncSnapshot` concept is a natural seed for versioned evidence. EU-facing SMEs and open-source stewards are the first audience, with deadlines in Dec 2026 (PLD), Dec 2027 (CRA, AI Act Annex III) and Aug 2028.
- **Don't build another trace store; be the map layer above traces.** Langfuse, Arize, Galileo and Helicone have been absorbed by ClickHouse, Dynatrace, Cisco and Mintlify. Ingest OTel GenAI spans (`invoke_agent`, tool calls) and Langfuse/Phoenix exports, and render them as a human-legible topology with causality. The gap is documented (only 27% address agent-decision visibility, 21% have runtime visibility), and OTel's agent conventions are still "Development", leaving a normalization window.
- **Add an agent capability/blast-radius graph as the headline feature.** Map agent → MCP servers/tools → credentials/secrets → environments (staging/prod) → data stores. PocketOS-style disasters and MCP tool-poisoning (actions that "look authorized in logs") are both failures of unmapped reach, and no law-driven competitor owns this view yet. Parse MCP configs, `.env`/token references, IaC and cloud IAM.
- **Overlay provenance without picking a standard.** Read `Co-Authored-By`, kernel-style `Assisted-by:`, PR metadata and agent session logs to color nodes by AI-authored vs human-reviewed and by which agent or model. Conventions are diverging (kernel simplifying, Kubernetes banning LLM trailers), so a neutral reader-and-visualizer has more value than an enforcer.
- **Position as an engineering tool with compliance exports, not a GRC platform.** Gartner sizes AI governance platforms at only about $492M (2026) to $1B+ (2030), while observability and security budgets are much larger.
